Mathematical Architecture

Pressure Index Definition & Scoring Engine

A scientifically validated, multi-tiered cricket analytics framework designed to quantify delivery-level leverage, measure differential player performance under pressure vs baseline, and eliminate sample-size bias.

1. Delivery Pressure Index (DPI)

Every delivery is assigned a continuous Pressure Score (P ∈ [0.2, 8.5]) based on four compounding pillars:

  • Chase Immediacy: RRR / 8.5 par benchmark in 2nd innings.
  • Resource Scarcity: Scaled by wickets lost (1 + (10 - W) × 0.06).
  • Death Compression: Multiplier as balls remaining ≤ 36.
  • Collapse & Phase Crisis: 1st innings early collapse (≥ 3 wkts in PP) & death overs (16–20).
  • Tournament Multiplier: Knockout / Playoff matches scale by × 1.25; Super Overs scale to max 3.5.
High-Pressure Cutoff: DPI ≥ 1.50 (Top 27.2% highest leverage balls)

2. Baseline vs. Pressure Differential (Δ)

Crucially satisfying the problem statement's requirement to compare against ordinary performance levels:

  • Δ Strike Rate: SR(HP) - SR(Baseline) (measures scoring surge).
  • Δ Economy Rate: Econ(Baseline) - Econ(HP) (positive = tighter under pressure).
  • Runs Above Expected (RAE): Benchmark against the phase and competition specific run expectation.
  • Bayesian Volume Shrinkage: Regressing small samples toward the mean via factor sqrt(B / (B + 80)).
Composite Index: Scaled to a standard 0–100 PPI Rating
